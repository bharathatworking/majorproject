## 2. Shift between calibration and test

- **Unfaithful share:** calibration 39/81 = 48.1%; test 34/119 = 28.6%. Catch and false-fail are per-class rates and do not depend on this share, but what a rejection means does: on the test set 60 of the 94 rejected outputs (63.8%) were faithful.
- **Injected vs real errors:** 20 of the 39 unfaithful calibration items are copies with one injected error (a changed number, a negation, a swapped name, a dropped fact, an added claim); the other 19 are real model outputs. All 34 unfaithful test items are real outputs of three models. In the table below the injected copies differ from real errors mostly on the NLI features (mean contra 0.434 vs 0.185, nliMin 0.202 vs 0.316); the test set's real errors look like the calibration's real ones (contra 0.210, nliMin 0.328). So about half the calibration errors were easier for NLI to see than the errors the checker meets in use.
- **Faithful items did not shift:** their mean FULL score is 0.526 on calibration and 0.535 on test, both below the frozen threshold 0.569168. The threshold was set for catch ≥ 90% on calibration, which already cost a high false-fail there (in-sample, at the frozen threshold: 76.2%, 32/42); the test set confirms that cost rather than revealing a new one.
- **Mean FULL score per group:** calibration, faithful 0.526; calibration, unfaithful (real outputs) 0.479; calibration, unfaithful (injected copies) 0.468; test, faithful 0.535; test, unfaithful (all real) 0.470. ## 4. Thesis paragraph

The pre-registered combined meaning score did not meet its targets on the held-out set. It ranks outputs better than the old hand-set rule (AUC 0.789 vs 0.656 for the old rule's 0/1 verdict), but at the frozen threshold it catches 100.0% of unfaithful outputs while failing 70.6% of faithful ones, and its correct/incorrect verdicts do not differ significantly from the old rule (exact McNemar p = 0.549). This is a negative result, reported as such: a small on-device checker (an extra-small NLI cross-encoder, all-minilm and entity matching, fit on 81 calibration items) cannot verify paragraph-level faithfulness with a usable false-fail rate. Post-hoc analysis (exploratory) shows that the high false-fail rate was already present in calibration (76.2% in-sample at the frozen threshold, the price of the pre-registered catch ≥ 90%), that faithful outputs score about the same in both sets, and that the calibration set (48.1% unfaithful, 20 of 39 of them injected errors) made errors look easier for NLI than the real ones of the test set (28.6% unfaithful). The false-fails have no single cause: they are spread over nliMin (17), lenRatio (15), sbert (14). The checker is therefore kept in the extension only as a cautious safety net: it rarely lets an unfaithful paragraph pass, it drives one retry, and the reader sees a neutral "Not verified" label with concrete reasons (a missing number, date or name, or a contradiction) rather than a failure. The meaning-preservation results of the thesis are judged offline by a larger model, not by this checker.
